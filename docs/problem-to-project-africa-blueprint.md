# Problem to Project Africa

## Founding Blueprint v1

### Working tagline
From local problems to concrete projects for Africa.

### One-line pitch
Problem to Project Africa is an AI-powered platform that helps African talents transform their skills, ideas, or observed local problems into concrete, relevant, and executable projects adapted to their country and reality.

---

## 1. Vision

Problem to Project Africa exists to help African talents stop asking:

- "What should I build?"
- "Which idea matters most?"
- "Is what I study useful for my country?"
- "How do I turn a problem into a real project?"

and start building projects that are:

- useful
- realistic
- locally relevant
- adapted to real constraints
- strong enough to improve careers, communities, and economies

Long-term, the platform should become a bridge between:

- talent
- local problems
- project execution
- collaboration
- opportunity
- and eventually funding

---

## 2. Core Mission

Help African students, developers, builders, entrepreneurs, and problem-solvers identify meaningful problems and turn them into projects with a clear roadmap.

This is not only a project idea generator.

It is a decision engine, a context engine, and an execution guide.

---

## 3. The Problem

Many African talents want to help their countries or communities, but they face one or more of these situations:

- they have skills but no idea what to build
- they have too many ideas and do not know what to prioritize
- they see a real-world problem but do not know how to structure a solution
- they study modern fields like AI, software, or data, but feel disconnected from the local needs of their country
- they want to build useful things, not empty portfolio projects
- they do not know which project is realistic given their level, time, and resources

This creates a painful gap:

- talent exists
- problems exist
- willingness to serve exists
- but the bridge between them is weak

Problem to Project Africa is meant to become that bridge.

---

## 4. Context and Philosophy

### 4.1 Reality-first approach

The platform must not blindly promote trendy technology.

It must ask:

- what is the actual local problem?
- how urgent is it?
- what are the real constraints?
- what type of solution is realistic?
- does this need AI, software, logistics, data, operations, or something simpler?

### 4.2 Technology is not the goal

Technology is only useful if it serves reality.

For many African countries, especially in early phases of development priorities, major challenges still include:

- food security
- electricity
- logistics
- health access
- education quality
- infrastructure gaps
- administrative friction
- low connectivity
- low purchasing power
- harsh physical conditions such as heat and dust

So the platform must not encourage disconnected "cool tech" projects.
It must encourage contextualized projects.

### 4.3 Build for home

The product should especially resonate with:

- African students in the diaspora
- young African developers on the continent
- graduates who want to build locally relevant solutions
- people with ambition but no clear project direction

The platform gives them a way to align:

- their competence
- their ambition
- and the needs of their environment

## 5. Who This Product Is For

### Primary users

- African students in computer science, engineering, product, business, agriculture, or technical disciplines
- African diaspora students who want to build useful things for their home country
- young developers who want stronger GitHub and portfolio projects
- early entrepreneurs who want to choose better project directions
- people who observe a problem and want help translating it into a project

### Secondary users

- junior professionals with side-project ambitions
- startup founders exploring local opportunity spaces
- communities or associations looking for practical project ideas
- incubators or mentors who want structured project suggestions for youth

### Future users

- collaborators looking to join meaningful projects
- recruiters scouting problem-solving talent
- investors looking at validated local opportunity spaces
- institutions interested in local innovation pipelines

---

## 6. User Types

There are three main starting modes.

### Type A: "I have skills but no idea"

This user says:

- I know web dev, data, AI, product, or agriculture
- I want to build something meaningful
- I do not know what to choose

The platform should turn skills into project directions.

### Type B: "I have an idea but need clarity"

This user says:

- I already have an idea
- I do not know if it is relevant
- I do not know if I should pursue it
- I do not know how to structure it

The platform should evaluate and refine the idea.

### Type C: "I see a problem"

This user says:

- I have noticed a real problem in society
- I do not know what project should come out of it
- I need help translating it into a concrete initiative

The platform should transform the problem into a project.

## 7. Product Promise

In a few minutes, Problem to Project Africa should help a user move from confusion to clarity by providing:

- locally relevant problems
- realistic project recommendations
- prioritization logic
- feasibility signals
- a roadmap to get started

The output should feel like:

- a strategist
- a product mentor
- a local context guide
- and a technical planner

working together.

The first version must stay focused.

- easier knowledge building
- clearer output quality

- Mali
- Niger

- Senegal
- and wider Africa

### Target user scope for v1

- students
- recent graduates
- junior builders
- early entrepreneurs

### Core value of v1

The platform returns:

- relevant problems
- project options
- one recommended project
- a practical roadmap

### What v1 will not do

Not yet:

- full social network
- investor marketplace
- team collaboration marketplace
- deep workflow automation
- government-grade advisory engine

## 10. Main User Flows

### Flow 1: Skill to project

Input:

- country
- domain
- current skill level
- existing tools/stack
- time available
- goal

Output:

- useful project ideas
- best recommendation
- roadmap

### Flow 2: Idea to validated project

Input:

- raw idea
- target users
- local context
- known constraints

Output:

- refined project concept
- relevance score
- feasibility score
- risk analysis
- next steps

### Flow 3: Problem to project

Input:

- problem description
- where it happens
- who is affected
- current failed solutions
- available resources

Output:

- project concept
- type of solution
- recommended scope
- implementation roadmap

## 12. AI Logic

The AI engine should not behave like a generic assistant.

It should behave like a constrained recommendation engine with local context.

### Inputs

- country
- profile type
- experience level
- domain
- stack or expertise
- sector of interest
- available time
- goal
- optional idea
- optional problem statement

### Outputs

- problem reframing
- project suggestions
- project recommendation
- impact estimate
- feasibility estimate
- roadmap
- skills gap
- first action step

### Evaluation dimensions

Each recommendation can be scored across:

- local relevance
- urgency
- feasibility
- cost to start
- required skill level
- execution complexity
- portfolio value
- business potential
- long-term expansion potential

## 13. Local Context Engine

This is one of the product’s biggest differentiators.

For each country, the system should progressively accumulate structured knowledge about:

- economic realities
- infrastructure constraints
- sector priorities
- cultural realities
- practical implementation challenges
- common pain points
- opportunity areas

### For Burkina Faso v1, the knowledge base should eventually include:

- national context basics
- major societal needs
- key sectors
- urban vs rural differences
- digital access realities
- education realities
- agriculture realities
- electricity and infrastructure constraints
- common entrepreneurship bottlenecks

Important:

This does not mean training a huge model from scratch.
In early versions, this can be a structured context layer:

- curated documents
- heuristics
- country prompts
- rules
- domain mappings

---

## 14. Sectors to Support in v1

To stay grounded, start with a small number of sectors:

- agriculture
- education
- health
- commerce and informal economy
- energy and logistics

Why these:

- they are highly relevant
- they are understandable
- they connect directly to real local needs
- they allow both digital and non-digital project paths

## 20. Build Principle

Do not try to build the final ecosystem first.

Build the smallest version that proves one thing:

Can we reliably help a user go from confusion to a relevant, executable project?

If the answer becomes yes, the rest can grow on top of it.
